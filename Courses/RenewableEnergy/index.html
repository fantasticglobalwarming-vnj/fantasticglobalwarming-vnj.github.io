Module 1 – What Is Renewable Energy?

Renewable energy refers to energy obtained from natural sources that are continuously replenished by natural processes. These sources include sunlight, wind, flowing water, geothermal heat, and biological materials. Unlike fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and gas, renewable resources do not run out when used responsibly. Renewable energy can be converted into electricity, heat, and fuel for transportation. Because it relies on natural cycles, it produces very low greenhouse gas emissions during operation. As global energy demand increases, renewable energy is essential for building a sustainable, secure, and resilient energy system.

Module 2 – Why Renewable Energy Matters

Renewable energy matters because modern society depends heavily on fossil fuels, which are finite and major sources of air pollution and climate change. Burning fossil fuels releases large amounts of carbon dioxide and other pollutants. By switching to renewable energy, countries can significantly reduce air pollution, improve public health, and slow climate change. Renewables also strengthen energy security by reducing dependence on imported fuels and volatile fuel prices. In the long term, renewable energy supports economic stability, environmental protection, and social well-being.

Module 3 – Types of Renewable Energy

There are several major types of renewable energy. Solar energy captures sunlight to generate electricity or heat. Wind energy uses moving air to spin turbines and produce power. Hydropower relies on flowing or falling water, geothermal energy uses heat from Earth’s interior, and biomass energy comes from organic materials such as plants and waste. Different regions use different renewable sources depending on geography, climate, and available resources.

Module 4 – How Renewable Energy Works

Renewable energy technologies convert natural energy flows into usable electricity or heat. Solar panels use the photovoltaic effect to turn sunlight into electricity. Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy of wind into mechanical motion, which is then transformed into electricity. Hydropower systems use moving water to rotate turbines. Once installed, these systems operate with low operating costs and no fuel combustion.
