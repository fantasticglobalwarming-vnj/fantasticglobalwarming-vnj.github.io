Module 1 – What Is Renewable Energy?

Renewable energy comes from natural sources that are replenished continuously, such as sunlight, wind, water, and geothermal heat. Unlike fossil fuels, renewable energy sources do not run out when used. These energy sources can be harnessed to produce electricity, heat buildings, and power transportation while producing very low emissions. Renewable energy plays a key role in creating a sustainable and secure energy system.

Module 2 – Why Renewable Energy Matters

Renewable energy matters because it helps reduce dependence on fossil fuels, which are finite and polluting. Using renewables lowers air pollution and supports climate stability. It also improves energy security by reducing reliance on imported fuels and stabilizing energy prices. Transitioning to renewable energy supports long-term economic and environmental resilience.

Module 3 – Types of Renewable Energy

Renewable energy includes several major types. Solar energy converts sunlight into electricity. Wind energy uses moving air to generate power. Hydropower relies on flowing water, while geothermal energy uses heat from Earth’s interior. Biomass comes from organic materials. Each type has different advantages depending on location and use.

Module 4 – How Renewable Energy Works

Renewable energy technologies convert natural energy flows into usable electricity or heat. Solar panels use the photovoltaic effect to generate electricity. Wind turbines convert kinetic energy from wind into mechanical rotation and then electricity. Hydropower turbines work similarly using moving water. These systems operate with minimal ongoing fuel costs.
